    Finnish

    [edit]

    Etymology

    [edit]

    Proto-Finnic *mala +‎ -o, borrowed from Baltic (compare Latvian mala, Lithuanian malá). The original senses are “shore, coast” and “edge, side”. The senses “hole, opening, mouth” and “crack, fissure, gap” are later developments.

    Pronunciation

    [edit]
    • IPA(key): /ˈmɑlo/, [ˈmɑ̝lo̞]
    • Rhymes: -ɑlo
    • Syllabification(key): ma‧lo
    • Hyphenation(key): ma‧lo

    Noun

    [edit]

    malo

    1. (archaic or dialectal) crack, fissure, gap
    2. (archaic, folk poetic) hole, opening, mouth
    3. (obsolete) edge, side
    4. (obsolete) shore, coast

      Spanish malo

        Inherited from Old Spanish malo, from Latin malus. Cognate with Ladino malo, Italian malo, Galician mao, Portuguese mau.

        Pronunciation

        [edit]
        • IPA(key): /ˈmalo/ [ˈma.lo]
        • Audio (León, Spain):(file)
        • Rhymes: -alo
        • Syllabification: ma‧lo

        Adjective

        [edit]

        malo (masculine singular before a noun mal, feminine mala, masculine plural malos, feminine plural malas, comparative peor or más malo, superlative malísimo)

        1. (with ser or (chiefly Latin America, sometimes) estar) bad (of bad quality)
          Antonym: bueno
          1. (with ser) bad (at/with something) [with para or en or con (see usage notes) or gerund]
            Synonym: also translated with dársele mal
            Antonym: bueno
            El profesor era malo y no sabía dar clase.
            The teacher was bad (at his job) and didn't how to teach.
            Soy malo con los números. ― I'm bad at numbers.
            Soy malo con las mujeres. ― I'm bad with women.
            Soy malo para el inglés. ― I'm bad at English.
            Soy malo en ajedrez. ― I'm bad at chess.
        2. (with estar, chiefly Latin America) bad (in a bad state)
          Synonym: mal
          Antonyms: bueno, bien
        3. (with ser) bad (morally), evil, mean
          Synonyms: malvado, infame
          Antonyms: bueno, bondadoso
          El profesor era malo y reprobó a toda la clase.
          The teacher was evil and flunked the whole class.
          mala persona ― bad person
        4. (as ser malo con, see usage notes) to treat badly, be mean/cruel to
          Antonym: ser bueno con
        5. (with ser) harmful, bad
          Synonyms: dañino, nocivo
          Antonyms: bueno, sano, saludable
          Fumar es malo para la salud. ― Smoking is bad for your health.
        6. (with estar) (of food) tasting bad
          Synonym: also translated with saber mal
          Antonyms: bueno, also translated with saber bien
        7. (with estar) (of food) bad, spoiled, rotten
          Antonym: bueno
          Esa manzana está mala, tírala. ― That apple's bad, throw it in the trash.
        8. (with estar) sick, ill
          Synonyms: mal, enfermo
          Antonyms: bien, bueno, aliviado, mejorado, sano

        Usage notes

        [edit]
        • When used before a masculine noun as part of the noun phrase, the apocopic form mal is used instead of malo.
        • "Ser malo con algo" can mean one of two things depending on context: "being bad at something" and "treating something badly/being mean to something".
        • "Ser malo en", "ser malo para", and "ser malo con" are used with different words in the sense of "be good at". Below is a general guideline (the words in parentheses are examples):
        1. malo en:
          1. Games and sports ((el) ajedrez (chess), (el) billar (pool), (el*) Fortnite, (el) fútbol (soccer/football), (el) esquí (skiing), (los) deportes (sports), (los) obbys)
          2. Domains of study, knowledge or practice (including school subjects) ((las) matemáticas/(la) matemática (math), (el) inglés (English), física (physics), geografía (geography), historia (history))
          3. With infinitives: "Soy muy malo en hacer videos" ("I'm very bad at making videos")
          4. With pronouns like "everything", "nothing", etc.: "No soy malo en nada" ("I'm not bad at anything"), "Soy malo en lo que hago" ("I'm bad at what I do") ("No soy malo con nada" is also possible but only if "nada" refers to a word that would use "con", "malo en/para nada" is the default)
          5. (en can also be used to mean "in" in a sentence that's like "I am bad [at X thing (implied)] [when I'm] in Y place", like "Soy malo en la cocina" ("I'm bad at cooking (lit. I'm bad in the kitchen, cooking is implied)"))
        2. malo con (usually with something before the word such as a definite article):
          1. The rest (el/los volante(s) (the steering wheel/steering wheels), el/los carro(s) (car(s)) las emociones (emotions), el piano (the piano), el balón (the ball), la(s) pistola(s) (the gun/guns), la(s) espada(s) (the sword/swords), la(s) computadora(s) (the computer/computers), la(s) pantalla(s) (the screen/screens), el/los pie(s) (the foot/feet), la(s) pierna(s) (the leg/legs), la(s) mano(s) (the hand/hands), el/los brazo(s) (the arm/arms), el/los cliente(s) (the customer/customers: bad at communicating with them, etc.), el/los niño(s) (the kid/kids: bad at taking care of them, etc.), los números (numbers, counting, math), las fechas (dates), las palabras (words), los nombres ((remembering) names), las banderas (flags (knowing them))
          2. (Latin America) Anything in 1.1 and 1.2
        3. malo para:
          1. Anything you would use malo en or malo con for except 1.5 and parts of the body
        *with optional article only colloquially
        • An alternative to malo en and malo para with an infinitive is to use a gerund: "Soy malo haciendo eso" ("I am bad at that")

        Ternate

        [edit]

        Pronunciation

        [edit]
        • IPA(key): [ˈma.lo]

        Verb

        [edit]

        malo

        1. (impersonal) to not exist
          bira malo ― there is no rice/rice does not exist
          ngogu malo toma hito ― there is no food in the kitchen
        2. (impersonal) to not have
          ngori pipi malo ― I have no money (literally, “there is no money of mine”)
        3. (intransitive) to not exist
          nyao imalo toma hito ― there is no fish in the kitchen
          mina momalo toma fala ― she is not at home

        Usage notes

        [edit]

        This word negates the verb of existence sema. In impersonal usage (unlike sema), malo generally follows the object of the sentence; in intransitive usage, malo follows the subject and precedes the object. To negate the transitive usage of sema, the impersonal form of malo is used.
