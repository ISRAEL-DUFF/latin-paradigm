Latin

[edit]

Etymology

[edit]
Etymology tree
Proto-Indo-European *weyh₁-?
Proto-Indo-European *wiHrós
Proto-Italic *wiros
Latin vir
Proto-Indo-European *-tuHts
Latin -tūs
Latin virtūs

    From vir (“man, male”) + -tūs (abstract noun-forming suffix).

    Pronunciation

    [edit]
    • (Classical Latin) IPA(key): [ˈwɪr.tuːs]
    • (modern Italianate Ecclesiastical) IPA(key): [ˈvir.tus]

    Noun

    [edit]

    virtūs f (genitive virtūtis); third declension

    1. manhood, manliness, virility
    2. courage, valor, resoluteness, gallantry
      Synonyms: spīritus, fortitūdō, fīdūcia, animus
      • c. 52 BCE, Julius Caesar, Commentarii de bello Gallico 1.1:
        Quā dē causā Helvētiī quoque reliquōs Gallōs virtūte praecēdunt, quod ferē cotīdiānīs proeliīs cum Germānīs contendunt, cum aut suīs fīnibus eōs prohibent aut ipsī in eōrum fīnibus bellum gerunt.
        • Translation by W. A. McDevitte and W. S. Bohn
          For which reason the Helvetii also surpass the rest of the Gauls in valor, as they contend with the Germans in almost daily battles, when they either repel them from their own territories, or themselves wage war on their frontiers.
    3. virtue, goodness
      Synonyms: bonum, rēctum
      Antonyms: vitium, culpa, malum, iniūria, noxa, peccātum, dēlīctum, error
    4. excellence, merit, worth, character
    5. (figuratively) strength (of medicine or herbs)
