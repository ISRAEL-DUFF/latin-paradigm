Latin

[edit]

Alternative forms

[edit]
  • pulcer
  • polcher, polcer (earlier)

Etymology

[edit]

Uncertain. The earlier form polcer is traditionally derived from Proto-Italic *porkros, from Proto-Indo-European *pr̥ḱ-ró-s or Proto-Indo-European *perḱ-ro-, from the root *perḱ- (“motley, variegated”), with dissimilation of the first *-r- into *-l-.[1][2] De Vaan has questioned this change, thinking that the second *r should have disappeared instead, and thus leaves this term without an etymology.[3] Connection with poliō (“to polish, smooth”) or polleō (“to be strong”) is to be excluded.[2] The aspiration is often considered secondary when the word would have been made to derive from Ancient Greek πολύχροος (polúkhroos, “multicolor”) by folk etymology,[n 1][5][4] while an Etruscan borrowing is usually discarded.[5][2]

Pronunciation

[edit]
  • (Classical Latin) IPA(key): [ˈpʊɫ.kʰɛr]
  • (modern Italianate Ecclesiastical) IPA(key): [ˈpul.ker]

Adjective

[edit]

pulcher (feminine pulchra, neuter pulchrum, comparative pulchrior, superlative pulcherrimus, adverb pulchrē); first/second-declension adjective (nominative masculine singular in -er)

  1. beautiful, fair, pretty
    Synonyms: bellus, speciōsus, fōrmōsus
    • c. 135-120 BCE, CIL 6.15346:[6]
      Heic est sepulcrum hau pulcrum pulcrai feminae
      Here is the ugly tomb of a beautiful woman
      (literally, “Here is the tomb of no beauty for a beautiful woman”)
  2. (figuratively) noble, honorable, excellent
    Synonyms: nōbilis, honōrābilis, excellēns
